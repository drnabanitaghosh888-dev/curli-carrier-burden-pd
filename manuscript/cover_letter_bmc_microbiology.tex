\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider our manuscript entitled ``Cross-cohort evidence synthesis identifies recurrent elevation of curli-carrier bacterial burden in Parkinson's disease gut metagenomes'' for publication as a Research Article in \textit{BMC Microbiology}.

This manuscript introduces Curli Carrier Burden (CCB), a novel and reproducible taxon-informed computational index for quantifying the aggregate abundance of curli-carrier bacterial taxa in processed gut metagenomic profiles. Curli is a bacterial amyloid with clear microbiological relevance and plausible connections to host amyloid biology, immune signaling, and gut-brain-axis research. We apply the CCB framework across public processed Parkinson's disease gut metagenomic cohorts and show recurrent PD-associated elevation, including statistically supported independent replication in Integrated-US and directionally supportive external evidence in Mao Central China and Romano non-Wallen analyses.

The study fits \textit{BMC Microbiology} because it focuses on microbial systems, bacterial amyloid ecology, reproducible microbiome analysis, and cross-cohort interpretation of public metagenomic profiles. The manuscript is conservative in interpretation: it does not claim causality, clinical diagnostic utility, curli gene validation, or raw-read validation. Instead, it provides a transparent computational framework and a microbiology-focused hypothesis-generating signal that supports future raw-read, gene-centric, and strain-resolved validation.

All analyses use existing public processed data and repository-derived outputs. Placeholder author, affiliation, funding, and reference details will be finalized before submission.
